Oversized Unisex Hoodie Fit for Women: Where the Cut Actually Sits

You order a small, it turns up, and the shoulder seam is sitting somewhere around your bicep. Nothing's gone wrong. That's just the cut, because there's no women's pattern behind this oversized fleece hoodie at all, only one unisex block graded from XXS upward, which means nobody ever drew it around a narrower shoulder or a higher bust point.

So the real question isn't what size you are. It's how much room you want to carry, and where that room lands on a frame that's shorter through the body than the pattern assumes.

Thirteen inches of room is not a grading error

Pull up the brand's own hoodie chart and a Medium measures 53 inches around the full chest. Somebody reaching for a Medium usually has a body chest of 38 to 40 inches. Subtract, and you're wearing roughly 13 to 15 inches of ease. Thirteen inches. That's what oversized actually means here, and it explains nearly everything else about how the hoodie sits.

The grading is tidy, at least: two inches of chest, one inch of body length and one inch of sleeve per size.

Charts disagree above Medium, so read the bigger sizes as approximate, and no garment chart exists per season — measurements shift between collections and nothing version-by-version has ever been published. The cm chart on the shop's own pages is a different animal again: S at 96–101 cm chest, M 102–107, L 108–113, XL 114–119. Those are your body measurements, not the hoodie's. People compare the two sets, find a 30 cm gap and panic. Don't.

Where the seams land if you are around 163 cm

The shoulder seam drops down your upper arm instead of sitting on the bone. No shoulder-drop figure has ever been published, so treat it as a look rather than a number, but on a narrower frame it travels further than intended and the sleeve head ends up near mid-bicep. Sleeves run long past the wrist. The armhole drops low too, which is why raising your arms lifts the whole hem.

And the hem, drafted to fall past the hip, often lands closer to mid-thigh if you're under about 5'4". Three things fix most of that without any tailoring:

  • Cuff the sleeves once or twice and let the ribbing bunch — it gives your wrists back.
  • Half tuck the front hem so the mid-thigh length reads as a choice. Marie Claire's round-up of hoodie outfit ideas leans on the same trick more than once.
  • Keep the bottom half fitted, so the volume stays on top where it belongs.

Length decides it, chest almost never does

With 13 inches of ease in play, chest measurement stops mattering. You'll fit into an XS, an S and an M, all three, and none will feel tight. Body length separates them — one inch per size, measured from the high shoulder point straight down. That inch is the difference between a hem at your hip and a hem halfway down your thigh.

So measure a hoodie you already own and like. Lay it flat, tape from the shoulder top to the hem, find the closest row above.

The brand says size up. It also says size down.

This confuses people and honestly it should, because both instructions exist. The women's fit guide runs bust 30 inches at XXS to 42 at XXL, waist 22 to 34, hips 32 to 44, and says size up if you fall between two sizes. The sizing advice on the unisex hoodie says size down one from your usual size.

They're answering different questions. The fit guide covers body measurements on a women's-cut garment, where between-size means slightly snug and going up buys comfort. The size-down advice covers a garment already carrying 13 inches of ease, where your usual size lands as full-on oversized.

In practice, women in the unisex cut go one down for the oversized look, two or three down for something closer to fitted. A UK 12 often ends up at XS or S. Sources disagree on how far, so treat it as a range. Sleeves also run short relative to the body, which is another quiet argument for going down.

Narrow shoulders, wider hips, and what goes underneath

Here's the bit no chart covers. If your shoulders are narrow and your hips wider, the chest fits long before the shoulders do, and the hem lands at the widest point of your body. Same size, same hoodie, and it reads shapeless on one person and sharp on another.

So the bottom half does most of the work. A straight-leg or tapered pair of fleece sweatpants keeps the silhouette readable — volume up top, a clean line below. Wide baggy legs under a mid-thigh hoodie turn the outfit into one rectangle with no waist in it anywhere. Show a little ankle. Who What Wear's editors make the same case in their edit of hoodie outfits that read low-key elegant: almost every look keeps the lower half narrow.

Colour helps in a small way. Oatmeal is the most forgiving neutral going, since its undertone doesn't commit warm or cool. Cement and Stretch Limo do a similar job darker, though these names are season-specific rather than a permanent palette.

Frequently Asked Questions

I normally wear a medium. Where do I start?

A Small, most likely. One down gives the oversized look without the hem going past mid-thigh. XS if you want a conventional fit, though the shoulder seam creeps back to where it should be and loses some character.

Is there a women-specific version of this hoodie?

Not as a separate cut. There's a women's fit guide with body measurements, useful for between-size calls, but the fleece hoodie comes off the unisex block in every size from XXS to XXL.

Will sizing down two sort out the long sleeves?

Partly. You lose two inches of sleeve, but also two inches of body length and four of chest, and the dropped shoulder more or less disappears. Cuffing is the cheaper answer.

Do the measurements change from season to season?

They can, and nothing published tracks it. The grading logic holds steady, but base measurements have shifted across collections. Measure against a hoodie you own rather than a number you read last year.

Sizing a unisex cut for a narrower frame stops being guesswork once you ignore the chest column. Work out your length, pick the row, order the size that puts the hem where you want it.
